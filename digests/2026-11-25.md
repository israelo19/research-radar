# Research radar, 2026-11-25

## Read today

**[When Answering Is Administering: Permission Replies That Rewrite Coding-Agent Policy](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32195)**  
Agent privilege escalation · score 10/10 · arxiv  
The paper shows that a remote plugin or service can answer a coding-agent permission prompt with a policy update that deletes deny rules, allowing later secret access even inside a strict sandbox. It derives tight runtime-recording bounds for safe delegation and shows enforcing them blocks the attack.  
Why it matters to you: It directly studies how permission prompts and runtime policy updates can let coding agents escalate beyond intended access.  
Read it asking: What minimal audit metadata is needed for a runtime to distinguish operator, plugin, and remote-service policy edits so delegated answers cannot silently remove deny rules?

**[LLM Agent Authorization Across Context Compaction](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32194)**  
Agent privilege escalation · score 9/10 · arxiv  
The paper shows that context compaction in coding agents can break request-derived authorization by making model-written summaries look like user input. It demonstrates prompt-injection attacks that change what an agent is allowed to do and proposes an attribution-based fix.  
Why it matters to you: It is directly about runtime authorization and prompt-injection-driven privilege escalation in coding agents.  
Read it asking: How much of the agent's effective permission boundary is actually determined by the compactor's summary rather than the user's original request?

**[Eliciting sandbagged LLM capability via structural interventions on attention-head functional networks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31615)**  
Eval awareness · score 9/10 · arxiv  
The paper studies sandbagging by building functional graphs from attention-head activations and compares their structure under honest versus sandbagged behavior. It finds that sandbagging is associated with reduced connectivity within detected communities and shows that ablating the most changed attention heads can recover suppressed capabilities.  
Why it matters to you: It directly addresses sandbagging and methods for detecting or eliciting hidden capabilities, which is central to eval-awareness.  
Read it asking: Does this structural signature of sandbagging hold in realistic evaluation settings, or only in controlled sandbagging setups?
